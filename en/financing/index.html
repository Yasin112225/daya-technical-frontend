Innovative Financing

Financing structure and tokenization of economic interests

This page explains how a financing structure takes shape, what role documentation plays and under which conditions — and limits — tokenization of economic interests may be worth examining.

A financing need is not a financing structure

"How much capital is needed?" is only the first question. More important: at which stages and in what sequence is it required, which cash flow is expected to repay it, and who carries the risk at each stage.

A financing structure is a combination of instruments, commitments and conditions aligned with the project's stage, asset base and repayment capacity. Choosing a structure before clarifying these issues is premature.

The role of documentation

A feasibility study, revenue and cost assumptions, permit status and ownership structure are the basis of any serious assessment. Their absence does not prevent a start, but it limits the depth of analysis and the reliability of conclusions.

Contractual structures and economic interests

Many projects contain definable economic flows: energy sales revenue, operating rights, a share in an asset's income or benefits under a long-term contract. Before any step, it must be clear how that interest is defined, measured, transferred and — in case of dispute — resolved.

At a glance

  • Tokenization is a potential instrument, not the project's objective.
  • It cannot be implemented without a clear legal framework.
  • It requires technical, financial, accounting and regulatory feasibility work.
  • It neither creates new value nor guarantees liquidity.
  • It does not suit every project.

The concept

What tokenization of economic interests means

In this framing, a "token" is simply a record and transfer mechanism for rights and obligations tied to an economic interest. Its validity depends on the legal, contractual and operational backing of that interest, not on technology alone.

A definable economic interest
A clear legal and contractual structure
Registry, measurement and settlement
Compliance with regulatory requirements
Risk management and dispute resolution

When might it be worth exploring?

  • A clear, measurable economic flow within the project.
  • Rights, obligations and transfer restrictions can be defined precisely.
  • An executable accounting, reporting and settlement path exists.
  • The regulatory framework governing issuance and transfer is clear.
  • The structure fits the project stage and the team's execution capacity.

Clear limitations

  • Tokenization does not by itself create new economic value.
  • It provides no guarantee of liquidity or of buyers.
  • It cannot proceed without the required permits and legal framework.
  • It is not a substitute for economic and feasibility assessment.

Any assessment in this area requires independent legal review and compliance with the regulations of the project's jurisdiction before any implementation step.
